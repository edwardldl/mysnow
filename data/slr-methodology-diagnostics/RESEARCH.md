# Regional SLR failure taxonomy and literature review

> **Research only.** This note explains why candidate regions failed and why the diagnostic methodology changed. It does not authorize a model artifact or production use.

## Failure taxonomy

The earlier search produced two fundamentally different failure classes. A modeling change can address the second class, not the first.

| Region/search | Failure class | Evidence and conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Pacific Northwest | No cleared admissible labels | The audited operational networks did not establish independent fresh-snow depth plus fresh-snow SWE over one interval. CoCoRaHS was the only plausible direct-label source, but reuse/commercial-training authorization was not positively established. See `data/pnw-slr-research/pnw-blocker-report.json`. |
| Canadian Rockies | No multi-station independent labels | Four ordinary ECCC stations were entirely exact 10:1 and the fifth was partly exact 10:1. ECCC documents that ordinary-station snowfall water equivalent is commonly derived by dividing depth by ten; the one potentially non-circular station cannot support unseen-station validation. See `data/eccc/canadian-rockies/reports/decision.md`. |
| Japan | Inadequate spatial support | NIED Shinjo reports contain genuine paired daily fresh-snow depth/SWE, but only at one fixed station. They cannot validate regional transfer to an unseen station. Positive JMA snow-depth change remains an invalid proxy for fresh snowfall. |
| Northeast/New England | Genuine transfer failure | The original intercept hierarchy changed MAE from 4.913 to 4.946 on 80 untouched unseen-station events. Training aggregate log residual was slightly negative while test aggregate residual was positive, and held-out station means spanned both signs. A nearly uniform correction therefore had the wrong aggregate direction and could not represent station/regime heterogeneity. |
| Northern Appalachians alternate mask | Physical-label gate failure | The alternate selection did not produce independent, same-window fresh-depth/fresh-SWE labels, so modeling never started. See `data/northern-appalachians-slr-research/northern-appalachians-blocker-report.json`. |
| Great Lakes/Upper Midwest | No cleared admissible labels | Every candidate paired WESF row in the exact GHCN audit had source flag `N` (CoCoRaHS); PRCP and total-pack WESD are not substitutes for independently measured fresh-snow SWE. See `data/great-lakes-slr-research/great-lakes-blocker-report.json`. |
| Alaska | No cleared admissible labels | All 2,770 threshold-and-field-pass events retained source `N` on WESF and none had row-level separate-core provenance; zero admissible labels remained. See `data/alaska-slr-research/alaska-blocker-report.json`. |
| Intermountain West | Reproducibility failure | The exploratory audit could not be reproduced reliably enough to satisfy the acceptance gate. Its cached intermediates are not a validated training corpus and no artifact was retained. |
| Broad remaining-U.S. audit | No cleared admissible labels | The completed 239-station audit left 5,205 source-`N` near misses and 323 exact-10:1 rejections, with zero strict admissible events. This reinforces that broader geometry cannot repair missing provenance. |

## Diagnosed methodology weaknesses

1. **Intercept-only transfer was too coarse.** The old global → climate → H3 hierarchy mostly supplied Northeast events with broad intercepts (`global>climate` for 62/80 and `global>climate>region` for 18/80), despite strong held-out station heterogeneity.
2. **A regional artifact's `global` term is not a worldwide prior.** It is estimated from that artifact's training slice. Applying it outside supported regimes risks a confident, wrong-sign correction. The leave-one-region-out diagnostic helps on three development holdouts but worsens untouched Northeast, so no global fallback is justified.
3. **Raw event weighting exaggerates repeated observations.** Multiple events from one station or storm are not independent evidence. Candidate selection now compares event-, station-, storm-, and joint-balanced fits and uses both unseen-station and complete-storm folds.
4. **A single temperature summary discards relevant structure.** Vertical and near-surface thermal structure, precipitation/snowfall amount, elevation, baseline SLR, SWE proxy, and profile completeness contain information that a uniform residual intercept cannot represent. The diagnostic uses only predictors physically present in retained profiles and synthesizes neither humidity nor wind.
5. **Point estimates overstated certainty.** Station- and storm-cluster bootstrap intervals are now reported. The 80% grouped-crossfit intervals remain explicitly labeled empirical heuristics, not conformal guarantees.
6. **Hyperparameter selection needed stronger isolation.** Shared candidate settings are selected only from Rockies, Sierra, and Swiss training partitions using grouped CV. Fixed regional holdouts are evaluated once after choices freeze, and Northeast is excluded from all selection.

## Literature findings and implications

- **Baxter, Graves, and Moore (2005), _A Climatology of Snow-to-Liquid Ratio for the Contiguous United States_, Weather and Forecasting 20, 729–744, DOI `10.1175/WAF856.1`.** The 10:1 assumption is not broadly representative; SLR varies geographically and with in-cloud, subcloud, and surface processes. The paper also documents gauge undercatch, settling, mixed precipitation, and erroneous exact-10:1 reports as observation problems. **Implication:** retain a physical baseline, add physically available event covariates, and keep strict provenance/QC rather than learning a constant map. Source: https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/20/5/waf856_1.xml
- **Roebber, Bruening, Schultz, and Cortinas (2003), _Improving Snowfall Forecasting by Diagnosing Snow Density_, Weather and Forecasting 18, 264–287, DOI `10.1175/1520-0434(2003)018<0264:ISFBDS>2.0.CO;2`.** Their predictors include low-, middle-, and upper-level temperature and humidity plus surface wind and liquid equivalent, reflecting microphysical and compaction controls. **Implication:** a profile-aware residual model is scientifically better motivated than an intercept-only correction; unavailable humidity/wind must be treated as missing capability, not synthesized.
- **Grossi et al. (2017), _Snow Precipitation Measured by Gauges: Systematic Error Estimation and Data Series Correction in the Central Italian Alps_, Water 9, 461, DOI `10.3390/w9070461`.** Unshielded gauges underestimated fresh SWE by 15–66% in the examined Alpine sites; paired manual depth/density measurements had an approximately 7% coefficient of variation. **Implication:** label and station uncertainty can dominate small model differences, so direct fresh-snow measurements and cluster-aware uncertainty are mandatory. Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/9/7/461
- **Veals et al. (2025), _Predicting Snow-to-Liquid Ratio in the Mountains of the Western United States_, Weather and Forecasting, DOI `10.1175/WAF-D-24-0233.1`.** Fourteen mountain sites supported multiple-linear-regression and random-forest models; temperature and wind already improved on legacy methods, while added humidity, solar, instability, and QPF predictors improved further. The study also explains why total-snowpack depth/SWE changes are confounded by compaction and why gauge undercatch worsens with wind. **Implication:** MySnow's full available-profile ridge is a defensible low-complexity ablation, but a validated production model still needs broader direct labels and retained humidity/wind predictors. Source: https://www.inscc.utah.edu/~u0813947/sharing/Veals_etal_2025_v1.5.pdf
- **NOAA Institutional Repository, _Snow-to-Liquid Ratio Prediction over the Contiguous United States Using Machine Learning_ (Weather and Forecasting 41, 317–338).** A random forest using CoCoRaHS labels and ERA5 profiles reported CONUS MAE 2.92 versus 3.51–4.76 for operational methods, while retaining difficulty at extreme SLR. **Implication:** atmospheric covariates can improve transfer, but this does not resolve MySnow's independent authorization review for CoCoRaHS-derived training labels or eliminate extreme-regime uncertainty. Source: http://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/73136

## Methodological decision

The diagnostic evidence supports grouped, physically covariate-aware candidate evaluation. It does **not** yet support replacing any retained artifact, enabling production, or creating a worldwide fallback. Any v2 artifact still requires cleared label rights, independent multi-station validation, forward/operational profile validation, and interval calibration under a defensible exchangeability unit.
